# Idler Homestead — Game Design Document

> **Status:** v1.0, ready for implementation. See [§15 Decision log](#15-decision-log).
> Remaining **[OPEN]** items (§14) are non-blocking and get decided when their milestone comes up.

## 1. Elevator pitch

You drove your electric pickup as far from everything as its battery would take you, and it died on a messy, overgrown patch of land in early spring. You have no money, a few seeds, and basic tools. Clear the land, haul water, plant, fish, and build up solar and wind power: first to survive the winter, then to grow the homestead into a self-sustaining, automated place that can take whatever the next year brings.

You start out knowing nothing. Everything you experience goes into your **Field Journal**, and that knowledge outlasts every failed winter.

Play hands-on, or step away and the homesteader keeps working. Over time the homestead turns from a chore-driven survival scramble into a quiet machine that mostly runs itself, small enough to sit in a corner of your desktop.

**Genre:** Idle / incremental with roguelite meta-progression and survival pressure.
**Setting:** Modern off-grid. Solar panels, wind turbines, battery banks, a dead electric truck.
**Mood:** Quiet, solitary, natural. Cozy in the warm months, tense when winter comes.
**Platforms:** Windows first. Mac, Linux, Android, iOS, and web kept viable from day one.

## 2. Reference games: what to take from each

| Game | Take | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| **Scritchy Scratchy** | Every action starts manual, then gets bought away (Scratch Bot, gadgets). Even once automated, doing it yourself still helps. Each run starts faster than the last. | Players report a plateau late in the game and say moving cards onto the auto-scratcher is tedious. Automation should never create *new* busywork. |
| **Cast n Chill** | Active and idle play are both first-class. Leave the controls alone and the game plays itself. A satisfying fishing loop with a fish guide to complete. Sparse music, nature soundscape. | Can feel empty once everything is found. Escalating years keep goals fresh. |
| **The Farmer Was Replaced** | Automation is the progression. It's satisfying to watch your system run. | Full programming is a steep wall. Borrow the *feeling* of building a system, not a code editor (§7.4). |
| **Ropuka's Idle Island** | Desktop ornament. Idling earns steadily, but *improving* the rate requires manual upgrades. | Weeks-long pacing. |
| **Frostpunk / Age of Empires** | Top-down settlement view. A known catastrophe on the horizon; the storm tests every earlier decision. | Grim moral choices, large populations. We're one person. |
| **Stardew Valley** *(contrast)* | Farming rhythms people already know. Top-down 3/4 view. | Social sim and town life. **We are isolated.** |

## 3. Design pillars

1. **Hands-on → hands-off.** Every chore starts as manual work. Progression means automating chores away: first by priorities, then by machines.
2. **Winter is the gate.** The first winter is the big test. Surviving it moves the game into its second half: scaling, automation, and harder years.
3. **Knowledge is the only thing you keep.** Gear, money, and land reset. What you've *learned* persists, and it's what lets you get back to where you were faster.
4. **Quiet isolation.** Nature, weather, solitude, a cat. Town is far away and getting there costs power.
5. **Idle anytime, active is better.** Let go at any time and the homestead keeps earning. Playing actively earns more, and only the player makes decisions.
6. **Lean into randomness.** Land, weather, threats, catalog specials, and finds all vary per run. Pull back where it hurts.
7. **Layout matters.** The homesteader walks everywhere, and the land is different every run.
8. **Show, don't tell.** Menus are icons, short labels, and visual links. No walls of text. Flavor text is always optional.
9. **Modular by construction.** Systems talk through shared interfaces; content and tuning are data. *(See §12.)*

## 4. Core structure: the run

### 4.1 Shape of a run: years with escalating challenge

A run lasts as many years as you survive. There is no "win screen". Each year asks more of you.

```
 YEAR 1: survival
 SPRING ─► SUMMER ─► AUTUMN ─► WINTER (the gate) ─┬─ fail ─► Rescue ─► Field Journal ─► new land, new run
                                                  │
 YEAR 2+: scaling                                 ▼
 SPRING ─► SUMMER (+ threat) ─► AUTUMN ─► WINTER (harsher) ─► ... until you fail
```

| Phase | Player focus | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| **Year 1** | Survive. Clear the land, water, first crops, fishing, wood for the winter, charge the truck, first sales. | The first winter is the big wall. Many first runs end here. |
| **Year 2+** | Grow and scale: more fields, livestock, a bigger power grid, machines, longer chains (smoking, canning, dairy, cattle). | Each year adds a **growing-season threat** (drought, locusts, blight, hail, early frost) and a harsher winter. *Built (D67): year 1 gets one gentle threat from summer on; later years more and stronger ones, see §7.9.* |

| Season | Role |
|---|---|
| **Spring** | Clear the land, establish, plant. |
| **Summer** | Grow. Long days = strong solar = more truck trips. Year 2+: threats hit here. |
| **Autumn** | Harvest and stockpile: smoke fish, preserve, cut wood and store it dry, insulate. The **winter forecast** is revealed in pieces. |
| **Winter** | Survive: daily draw on food, heat, and water. No outdoor crops. Weak solar, so truck trips are rare and deliveries can be delayed by snow. Ice fishing. Long nights = **study season**. A **cold-snap peak**, like Frostpunk's storm. |

**Pacing:** ~15 real minutes per season at 1× was the original target (~1 hour for Year 1); since D56 a day is 320 s and a season about 27 min with fast sleep. All season lengths are tuning values (§12.3).

### 4.2 Surviving vs. failing

- **Needs** draw from stockpiles every day, all year: **Food** always, **Warmth** on the cold nights, none in summer, partial in spring and autumn, the most in winter (wood / electric heat, D104), **Water** (pipes and wells freeze without heat or insulation). Animals add their own needs.
- *Built (M3):* food and warmth are drawn each morning for the day/night just passed (`src/sim/systems/needs.lua`); the winter's severity and cold snap scale the warmth need (D36, D37, D104).
- *Built (#95, D160):* **drinking water** is a daily draw too (`Needs.drink`, `Needs.waterNeeded` units a day, `tuning.needs.dailyWater`). It comes from the ledger `water` stock first (the cistern, the same pool as irrigation), then from a **canteen** the homesteader fills by hand (`tuning.needs.canteenCapacity`, full at the start of a run). When both are short, **Thirst** builds slowly like the other needs: a dry day costs `thirstDamage` times the shortfall and each dry day in a row adds `thirstBuild` more, up to `thirstBuildDays`, so it never ends a run in one night; a thirsty day restores no Condition. The way out is always there: the standing task `fetch_drinking` fills the canteen at the creek (through the ice in winter) or a dug well whenever the days of water on hand fall under `drinkRefillDays`, and in a frozen season `melt_snow` melts a batch beside a fire for `meltWood` wood. `Needs.waterOutlook` (`days`, `stock`, `canteen`, `thirstDays`) is the figure the Tonight card, Footing's "water source" and Readiness read, like `warmthNeeded`. A **drink chip** on the top bar shows the days of water on hand, with its own modal, alert and morning-report line; the rescue names thirst as a cause. Freezing of the stock itself (pipes, wells, cisterns) is still the pipe-freeze work (#91, #211).
- *Built (#340, D161):* the **dusk check**. `tuning.night.duskLeadHours` (4) before sunset the **Tonight card** opens by itself, once a day, until bedtime: warmth tonight needs (`Needs.warmthNeeded`) against the wood in stock and the nights it covers (`Needs.woodOutlook`), food for tomorrow (`Needs.foodDays`), drinking water (`Needs.waterOutlook`), the shelter, an exposure line (a stub guessed from the shelter alone until C1) and the risks the forecast shows (a cold snap, a storm). Every figure comes from `Readiness.tonight(world)` and each row's Fine / Low / Short is the chips' own alert level, so the card never disagrees with the top bar. A gap has a one-tap fix that issues an ordinary order (`Dusk.fixes` / `Dusk.apply`): *Chop N trees* marks the nearest trees as Axe marks until their wood reaches the amber line, *Ask the routine to fish* puts Fish first in the routine, *Fill the canteen* orders the homesteader to the creek, a well or a fire; where nothing can be ordered the button opens the Catalog, the town menu or the Pantry. The card never blocks input (a tap outside it reaches the ground), closes with Close or Esc (and stays closed until tomorrow's dusk), and comes back from the **Tonight stamp** on the left edge (shown in the evening and whenever something is short) or the **T** key at any hour; Tab walks its buttons and Enter presses one. It is a side card on a wide view and a bottom sheet on a tall one, and it gives way to a lens. `Readiness.tonightDue` is the one place the Footing state (D162) switches the card off.
- *Built (#341, D162):* **Footing**, the state that moves the pressing goal from the night to the season (PLAN_DEPTH §3.2). `Footing` (a system, run state `world.state.footing`, so a new run starts without it) is judged once a day at dusk, the first tick at or after sunset. A *good night* holds all four: a shelter with a heat source (a camp with a campfire counts), a water source (`Needs.waterOutlook` days of drinking water in hand, `tuning.night.footing.waterDays`), `fuelNights` (3) nights of wood and `foodDays` (3) days of food. `streakNights` (5) good nights in a row earn it (`footing.gained`); any night that is not good starts the count again. Held, it **slips** (`footing.slipped`) when wood falls under `slipFuelNights` (1) night or food under `slipFoodDays` (1) day, and is **regained** (`footing.regained`) after `regainNights` (2) good nights in a row. While slipped `Footing.paused(world)` is true: the accessor the longer goals (contracts, charters, new bad events) read. D168 amends the intent: it means "slipped" and gates offers, not timers; clocks keep running and the levy is reduced, not paused. `Readiness.tonightDue` returns `leads` from `Footing.tonightLeads`: the Tonight card, stamp and auto-open lead until Footing is earned and again while it is slipped, and stay away otherwise (the T key still works). Gaining it shows a one-time card, a panel in the Tonight card's place (never blocks input, Close, Tab and Enter, phone sheet) with the four tests held and the slip rule, and writes the Field Journal note "Footing". Slip and regain give a toast.
- *Built (#342, D163):* the **goal ladder**. A tappable **goal line** sits under the top bar, first above the decision stamps (no top-bar chip): `Goal.current` names the pressing goal, Tonight's short rows until Footing (and again while it is slipped), then the Footing checklist and nights in a row, then the days to winter, the wood and food reserve target and the Great Winter's status. A tap or **P** opens the **Progress panel**: the goal, the year and season, the Footing tests, the run's **milestones** (`data/content/milestones.lua`, eight, ticked by world events into run state, `SAVE_COMPAT` 15) and the gates that open at Footing (a stand-in list until the gate framework, A4).
- *Built (#343, D164):* the **gate framework**. A catalog definition may carry `requiresFooting = true`; `Footing.isOpen(world, gate)` is the one accessor (open while Footing is held, slipped or not) and `Market.cannotBuy` answers `needs Footing`, so a flagged item is listed but cannot be ordered or bought until then. The Catalog row, the item card and the Journal card show "Opens at Footing" with a disabled "Locked" button; nothing is hidden. The Progress panel lists flagged items, then `tuning.progress.surfaces` for gated things with no content yet. No shipped item carries the flag.
- *Built (#345, D166):* **statuses**, the injury and condition model's sim (PLAN_DEPTH §7). A status (`data/content/statuses.lua`: ankle at three severities, fever, back, burn, tired) is a short list of modifiers on the homesteader, held in run state `world.state.status` (`SAVE_COMPAT` 16). **Families escalate, they do not stack** (`Status.apply`: a second status of an active family rises to its `escalatesTo`, the cap renews its time); different families coexist up to `tuning.status.maxActiveStatuses` (3). The combined work speed never goes under `minWorkSpeed` (0.35) and a daily `conditionDrain` never takes Condition under `drainFloor` (1). Modifiers reach the work rate (times the wardrobe's bonus), the bucket, `Actors.fallFactor`, `Needs.warmthNeeded`, `Needs.waterNeeded`, `Needs.daily` (recovery and drain) and the journal's study rate through `Stats.factor`. Events `status.applied` and `status.cleared`. Nothing applies a status yet (events come in B3).
- *Built (#346, D167):* **status chips and their card**. Each active status shows a chip under the Condition meter: its family icon, the main effect and the days left ("Work 20% slower · 1d", "until cured"; the name stands in when the effect does not fit). On a wide view the chips are a column right-aligned under the meter and the Tonight card starts below them; on a phone, where the vitals wrap, they are the first lines of the left stack above the goal line. The top plates are unchanged. A tap on a chip, or **H** (a rebindable action `status`, the first status), opens the card: every effect in words, the time left, what clears it, what happens if the status is hurt again and how many of the slots are used; Close, Esc or H closes it, and it closes itself when the status ends. The recovery scene adds "Hurt this run: ..." under the cause, from the run's status history (`world.state.status.history`, created with the first status, copied into the run summary as `hurt`).
- *Built (#347, D169):* **the event engine** (PLAN_DEPTH §5, §6.2, §6.3, §6.5). `Events` (a system, run state `world.state.events`, `SAVE_COMPAT` 17) draws once a day at dusk, from its own `event` RNG stream, whether tonight or the day after brings a misfortune: `tuning.events.chancePerNight` x the year x **exposure** (`Events.exposure`, 0 to 10 points from the shelter, the stores that attract animals and the livestock, `tuning.night.exposure`) plus a **pity** lift after `pityNights` quiet nights. One **night slot** is shared with the threats still to come (C1): `Events.claimSlot`, and `Events.slotOutcome` ("turned" resets the pity timer and starts no cooldown, "landed" also starts the post-bad cooldown). The **quiet start** is two rules: nothing is drawn before night 3, and no bad event comes before Footing is earned or day `badFromDay` (10), whichever comes first (Q10). A hit schedules one event by weight (season, terms, exposure) for its hour; when due it is offered into the **pending queue** (`Events.pending`, `choose`, `ignore`, `force`) unless a card is open or the player is away, after a last look at the mercy rules, the conditions and `perDayBudget`. **Time never waits**: the card holds `ignoreHours` of game time and at least `ignoreMinSeconds` of real time, both counted only while the player is present (the scene feeds `Events.advanceReal` and `Events.setAway`: unfocused, or past the takeover delay with no input); then the free ignore outcome applies. **Mercy**: nothing is offered under `lowCondition`; no bad event while Footing is slipped or the statuses are full; Condition never goes under `conditionFloor` (`Needs.injure`); `capital` and over-the-cap `damage` outcomes are swapped for their `lesser` form; `maxOpenDamage` caps damage flags; the warmth need that statuses add is capped at `maxWarmthMultiplier` times the season's base need, or what weather and shelter already ask (`Needs.warmthNeeded`), so a night with no event is exactly as before. Content (`data/content/events.lua`, schema in the file header): two options with cost, risk words and `careful`/`bold` flags, a separate `ignore`, and the effect vocabulary (status, gain, lose, condition, flags, damage, lockTiles, damageTiles, note, chain). `Events.audit` judges every ignore outcome (free, no Condition loss, nothing that can escalate or chain, never worse than the worse option) and runs in the tests. The first occurrence writes a Field Journal note (`Journal.write`, and `Journal.once` for a once-per-run flag, the API #109 asks for). The demo is the **sprained ankle** (Splint it for 2 wood: Hobbling; Power through: Strained; ignore: the free, non-escalating Limping for two days). Events: `event.offered`, `event.chosen`, `event.ignored`. The card (B4) is not built: toasts say what came and how it ended. The harness has `events=off|ignore|careful|bold|random` (off by default, so its output is unchanged). Not built: standing orders (B5), the other packs, the `human` policy and paired seeds, wake tiredness.
- *Built (#362, D171):* **winter wood is a stockpile problem** (PLAN_DEPTH F4 and C8, #65). A season listed in `tuning.wood.fellAllowance` (winter, 24 wood, about three trees) lets standing trees give only that much wood. `Felling` (a system, run state `world.state.felling` = { key, wood }, `SAVE_COMPAT` 18) adds the wood each felled tree gave to the season's tally; the check is made when a tree is started, so the last tree may pass the allowance, and the tally starts over with the next season (no event needed: it is keyed by year and season). Once it is used up the Axe refuses trees (the `clear` task's `limit` hook, a reason the scene turns into a toast), the routine's "Clear trees" category and the Axe marks skip them (the marks stay and resume next season), and a building ordered over a tree is held with a reason. Logs, brush, bought wood and the woodshed are untouched, so the only on-demand wood in a hard season is dead wood, and the rest of the winter's fuel must be in the store before it freezes. It is shown where the player plans: the Tonight card's warmth row ("Trees: 18 of 24 wood left this winter", or none left), the winter readiness card (from the autumn lead days: "Once it is winter, trees give only 24 wood in all"), the refusal toast and the Chop fix, which offers logs only while the allowance is used up. - If a need runs out, **Condition** (health bar) starts falling. If Condition hits zero, the run ends.

### 4.3 Failure: search & rescue → new land

1. **Rescue.** A search & rescue team finds you. Short scene: lights in the snow. You wake up recovering in town.
2. **Field Journal.** Review what you learned this run. Pick what to study during recovery (one free study, §8.3).
3. **New land.** You head out to a **new, randomly generated plot**. Nothing carries over except your knowledge and the starting gear and head starts it grants.
4. **Messy start.** Every new plot starts overgrown and cluttered: brush, rocks, fallen trees, junk. Clearing it is the run's natural **tutorial** and gives **starting resources** (wood, stone) and a **little money** (scrap and junk are worth a few dollars). Otherwise you start with **$0**.

**No salvage carries over from a failed run.** You get back faster because of what you've learned, and you only have that knowledge because the previous run got far.

*Built (M3):* `src/meta/run.lua` records each run in `meta.career` (run number, history of summaries), finishes the study in progress, and sets `meta.recovery` until the player heads out, so quitting mid-recovery resumes there. Scenes: `rescue` (lights in the snow; the rescuer's line changes after the first rescue) → `recovery` (run summary, notes earned this run, one free study) → `homestead` on new land.

### 4.4 Progress and the soft win

The game is **endless**: there is no hard win state, only "until you fail" (D117).

- **Headline metric: years survived.** The recovery screen shows the run's years against the slot's best and the last run.
- **Late-game metric: best year's income.** Money earned from sales in one game year, spending ignored. It is shown against the slot's best, so a run that has outgrown dying still has a number to beat.
- **Run milestones.** A short checklist per run (first winter, power, a shack, fed for a year), from existing events and notes, shown on a Progress panel. *Built (#342, D163):* eight milestones in `data/content/milestones.lua`, run state, ticked on the panel the goal line under the top bar opens (P key).
- **The Great Winter and "Established".** Winter of year N (a tuning value, 3 to start) is the hardest the run has seen, and is announced early. Surviving it to the thaw fires a one-time "Established" card and marks the slot. Play then continues as before. It has no mechanical reward.
- **Journal completion %** shows on the slot screen as the long-term meta bar.

## 5. Core loop

### 5.1 Moment to moment

```
 Do things ─► Harvest / catch ─► Eat · Preserve · Sell ─► $ ─► Buy from catalog ─► Better / automated work
     │                                                            ▲
     └─► Notes ─► Ideas ─► Study at night ─► Known ───────────────┘
```

### 5.2 Active vs. idle

**The rule:** idling grows you *linearly*. Active play is *more efficient*, and only the player can *raise the line*.

| | Active play | Idle |
|---|---|---|
| **Trigger** | Any input | After **N seconds** with no input (e.g. 30s; shortened by upgrades) |
| **Control** | **Click / tap** a target. The homesteader walks there and does it. | Homesteader follows the **priority list** (§7.4). |
| **Chores** | Faster, plus active bonuses (e.g. hand-watered crops grow a bit faster). | Done automatically at the base idle rate. |
| **Fishing** | The **tension mini-game** (§7.10). Rare fish possible. | Auto-fishes at a lower rate, common fish only. |
| **Decisions** (build, choose study, buy, food rules, plan fields beyond the starter, truck trips) | Player | **Never automated**, except the starter field when nothing is planned (D61) and the event rules the player has chosen (standing orders, D176: Ask me, Careful or Bold per event class, once the Rules of thumb perk is known). Pending decisions show as gentle notifications. |
| **Efficiency** | Base × active multiplier | Base × idle multiplier (raised by upgrades) |

Any input instantly hands control back. A **Start routine** button (key R) skips the wait and hands over at once (D43). Browsing a panel is not "input" for this timer, and a routine that was running at bedtime resumes at sunrise (D55). All multipliers live in tuning (§12.3) and will be iterated on heavily. When the routine takes over and nothing is planned, tilled or growing, it plans a small starter field (4 radish, 2 potato tiles by default) near the truck and water, so an unattended start does not starve (D61); it never touches a player-made plan.

### 5.3 Chores → automation ladder

| Chore | Rung 0 (manual) | Rung 1 | Rung 2 | Rung 3 (machines) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Water** | Haul buckets from the creek | Dug well + hand pump | Solar DC pump → cistern; **sprinkler** (D118) | Drip irrigation |
| **Crops** | Hoe, sow, water, harvest by hand | Better tools, raised beds | Irrigated beds, greenhouse | **Auto-harvester** (radius), **mini-tractor** |
| **Fishing** | Basic rod, mini-game | Better rods, reels, bait | Fish trap / trotline (passive) | Pond stocking |
| **Fuel / heat** | Gather deadfall, campfire | Axe, wood stove | Woodshed (dry storage, raises the wood cap), insulation | Heat pump (power-hungry in winter!) |
| **Food preservation** | Eat fresh, cooler | Root cellar, **smoker** | Solar dehydrator, canning | Chest freezer (power) |
| **Animals** | Hand-feed, hand-water | Feeders, waterers | Heated automatic waterer | Auto-feeder, egg collector |
| **Selling** | Truck trips only | **Roadside stand** (capped sales/day) | Stand upgrades (higher cap, cooler) | — |
| **Power** | Folding solar panel (trickle-charges the truck) | Fixed solar array | Battery bank, more panels | Wind turbine; micro-hydro; backup generator |
| **Shelter** | Sleep in the truck | **Camp** (lean-to), built D82 | **Shack** (insulation, desk, wood stove inside), built D82 | Cabin, then heated workshop (later, #198) |

*Built (#198 first slice, D82): the shelter ladder* (`src/sim/systems/shelter.lua`, accessors only; objects in `data/content/objects.lua`, shared knob in `data/tuning/shelter.lua`, journal nodes Camp and Shack on the Shelter page, notes "Cold cab" and "Canvas night"). A shelter is an object with `shelter = true` and the content fields `shelterRank`, `insulation` (multiplier on the night's warmth need), `recovery` (multiplier on the daily Condition recovery), `heaterRadius` (tiles) and, for a desk, `studySpeed`. **Insulation** scales the warmth need through `Needs.warmthNeeded`, which the electric heater's thermostat and winter readiness (`Readiness.warmthAhead`, `Readiness.winter`) read too, so the fuel chips and the keep target stay honest. **Heater slot**: a heat source (campfire, wood stove) or electric heater within `heaterRadius` tiles of the sleeping shelter delivers its warmth in full; farther ones deliver `tuning.shelter.farHeat` of it. **Bed choice**: `Sleep` walks to the highest-ranked reachable shelter (nearest breaks a tie); a tap-to-choose preference is not built.

**The winter power squeeze:** solar drops in winter just as heating, freezers, and pumps need more power, and you still want to charge the truck. This is our equivalent of Frostpunk's generator.

*Built (M5, #35): the water ladder* (`src/sim/systems/water.lua`; objects in `data/content/objects.lua`, knobs in `data/tuning/water.lua`, journal nodes on the Water & Soil page). Each rung removes more of the bucket chore, and the routine and hand hauling keep working at every rung (D50):
- **Rung 0, creek.** The bucket is filled at the water's edge (under ice, at a cut hole).
- **Rung 1, dug well and hand pump.** Both need a tile with a high **water table** (a seeded per-tile value, higher near the creek and in groundwater pockets away from it; wells and pumps are refused elsewhere, and the tiles are tinted while placing). They are closer places to fill the bucket: the dug well (rope and bucket) is a bit faster than the creek, the hand pump faster still, and neither needs ice cutting or power.
- **Rung 2, solar pump and cistern.** The pump is a power sink in the pumps tier; while there is room in the cistern it draws power and adds water at a rate scaled by how well the tier is served. The cistern gives the shared `water` stock its capacity and is itself a (fastest) place to fill the bucket, so the walk to the creek is gone once it is built.
- **Rung 2, sprinklers (D118).** Like a drip line, but cheap, unlocked with the cistern and twice as wasteful: it waters a 5x5 patch at a full bucket's worth of the stock per crop, against half for drip.
- **Rung 3, drip lines.** Not an actor: every tick a drip line waters thirsty crops within its radius from the cistern stock, at half a bucket-slot per crop, so a stocked line means no bucket trips at all (the **sprinkler**, D118, is the same patch at a full bucket-slot per crop, unlocked earlier). With the stock empty the zone is **dry** (red status light and zone tint, a toast, the object card says so) and the routine hauls by hand again.

## 6. Economy

### 6.1 Money vs. knowledge

| | **$ Money** | **Knowledge** |
|---|---|---|
| Is | What you *have* | What you *know* |
| Comes from | Selling goods (truck trips, roadside stand); a little from clearing junk | Doing things → Notes → Ideas → Study (§8) |
| Used for | Buying known items from the catalog | Deciding *what* is in the catalog, plus perks |
| Persists? | **No**, $0 each run | **Yes**, forever (per save slot) |

Knowledge is **not a currency**. It's never spent. You earn it by doing and choosing what to study.

### 6.2 Food flow: eat, preserve, or sell

```
                                    ┌─► KEPT ──────► eaten daily, burned at night, held for the smoker
 HARVEST / CATCH ─► fresh, spoils ──┼─► PRESERVE ──► smoked / canned / dried (keeps all winter; sells for more)
                                    └─► SPARE ─────► roadside stand (trickle) or loaded on the truck (a trip)
```

**Keep targets** (D75; replaced the sell crate and its rules): every good that can be eaten, burned or sold has **one keep number** in the Pantry. Stock above it is **spare**, and spare is what the roadside stand and the truck load sell. Nothing is moved: keep and spare are read against the stock, so food keeps aging and stays edible wherever it "is".
1. **Automatic by default**, worked out from the stock: fresh food fills **3 days of eating** (each perishable only for as long as it would be eaten before it spoils, D68), then **10 days for winter** in preserved food, potatoes and fresh food waiting for a placed smoker or press; wood covers every night to the end of the winters ahead (D104: the coming one, and the next; cautious where the forecast is silent); everything else (scrap, stone) is all spare.
2. **Set your own** with - and + on a row; **Auto** goes back. Food you keep by hand counts toward the 3 and 10 days first.
3. **Stand or Truck** per good: a good set to Truck never sells at the stand (D62).
4. **What is eaten first** (D94): each day's meal takes the food whose oldest batch would spoil soonest, then the next, so the radishes about to rot go before fresh fish; ties in content-file order, and food that never spoils (smoked, canned) last. Within one food the oldest batch always goes first.

Defaults are sensible, so a new player never has to touch this. The Pantry shows how long each perishable keeps and when its oldest batch spoils (D73).

### 6.3 The catalog

- A modern off-grid supply catalog. Items come in three states: **unknown**, **idea** (visible, not yet studied), and **known** (buyable).
- A small **starter page** is always known: seeds, basic tools, basic bait.
- **Order anytime, it gets delivered.** Deliveries arrive after a delay (e.g. next morning) at the **drop-off point** where your road meets the plot. Where you build storage relative to the road matters. Small delivery fee. Snow can delay winter deliveries.
- **Pickup only:** animals and oversized items (and trailer-dependent ones) must be collected on a truck trip.
- **Purchase limits (D114, D115):** every item is **unique** (one ever: the stand, buildings like the woodshed and stove, gear), an **upgrade** that replaces its predecessor (camp -> shack, basic -> sturdy rod, plain -> big bucket, the stand's sign, cooler and rack), **repeatable** (pens, cisterns, power gear, auto-harvester, pumps) or a **consumable**. An item you already own stays in the list labelled **Owned**; the full table is in D114.
- **Random specials:** a few discounted or rare items each week. Some are **in-store only** (a reason to drive).

### 6.4 Selling

| Channel | How it works | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Truck trip** | **Load the truck** with spare goods up to its cargo (40 units; Auto-load takes the best price per unit first, then what spoils soonest, never below a keep), drive to town (a menu), **Sell the load**; anything not loaded stays home (D75). Buy pickup-only items and in-store specials. No delivery fees. | Costs a big chunk of truck charge. Few trips early on. The big payday. |
| **Roadside stand** *(unlock)* | Sells **N items per day** of spare goods not loaded on the truck automatically, cheapest goods first (ties: what spoils soonest), at 60 percent of town price (D52). Idle-friendly income. A per-item "Truck" pin in the Pantry keeps a good off the stand (D62). | **Always capped.** Upgrades (sign, cooler, display) raise the cap but never remove it. |

Prices vary week to week. Some goods are in demand. Preserved goods sell for more than fresh.

**More power → more trips → more income.** Power is the economy's bottleneck.

## 7. Systems

### 7.1 Time & calendar
- Fixed-step simulation tick, separate from rendering. Time scale adjustable: a debug key, and a player speed button (paused / 1x / 2x / 4x, D42).
- **Day length is settled for M8 (D159):** 320 s per day at 1x (about 3.8 real minutes awake plus asleep, a season of 7 days about 27 minutes, a year of 28 days about 1 h 45). Sleep stays 8x, and study and the idle takeover delay are unchanged. Food, warmth and spoilage are per day. If the day is ever lengthened, task and walk times scale with it, so a day holds the same work at a slower pace.
- **No offline progress.** Time only passes while the game is open. Keeps running when unfocused or minimized (desktop). Mobile suspends, so time pauses.
- Day/night cycle, seasons, weather. Weather affects crops, solar, wind, fish activity, and threats. Day-to-day weather (clear, cloudy, rain, windy, storm) is a seeded, season-weighted run of spells with an exact few-day forecast (D51).
- **Nights** are when studying happens (§8.3).

### 7.2 Resources
- A single **ledger** of typed resources: `water`, `wood` (the only fuel, D104), `stone`, `scrap`, `seeds.*`, `crops.*`, `fish.*`, `preserved.*`, `eggs`, `milk`, `money`, `power`...
- **Storage limits** come from buildings (woodshed: the base wood cap is raised only by sheds, cellar, battery bank, freezer).
- **Spoilage** (D73): fresh food has a shelf life, tracked per batch (each harvest, catch or collection). Nothing spoils until a batch is older than its shelf life, then it rots over a few nights. Food is eaten and sold oldest first; a powered freezer stretches shelf lives; preserved food never spoils.

### 7.3 Land, grid & placement
- **New random land every run**, generated from a seed: creek course, ponds, soil quality, trees, rocks, water table, wind and sun exposure, road / drop-off position. Every plot has trade-offs.
- **Plot variety (D135).** Water is more than one creek: a tributary joins the main creek on most plots, and two to four still ponds sit apart from it (half of them marshes, with shallows round the rim and brush on the banks). The land is cut into **biome regions** (10 to 14 cells round random points, edges bent by noise, soft at the borders): oakwood, pinewood, birch grove, open meadow, scrub and rocky ground, each with its own trees, density, rocks, brush, clutter and soil, so a wood reads as a wood. The truck always sits in a meadow. Clearings (round gaps with a log on the rim) are cut into the woods.
- **Starts messy** (§4.3).
- **Tile grid.** The sim knows only grid coordinates.
- **Free placement on the grid:** fields are tile areas. Buildings have footprints and placement rules. Objects go on any buildable tile (grass or tilled soil; roads and water never), subject to their own rules; trees, rocks, logs, brush and crops in the way are cleared or removed first (D79).
- **Walking distance matters.**
- Buildings are composed from **components**: `Producer`, `Consumer`, `Storage`, `PowerSource`, `PowerSink`, `Battery`, `Upgradeable`, `Freezable`, `HeatSource`, `AreaOfEffect`, `Processor`, `Shop` (roadside stand)...

### 7.4 Tasks, actors & priorities (the systemic core)

Everything that needs doing is a **Task**. Everything that does work is an **Actor**. Manual play, idle play, and automation are the same system with different actors choosing tasks differently.

**Task categories:** `clear`, `water`, `plant`, `harvest`, `haul` (move goods to pantry / smoker / stand), `process`, `fuel`, `animals`, `fish`, `maintenance`.

| Actor | Chooses tasks by | Capabilities / area |
|---|---|---|
| Homesteader (**active**) | Player click/tap | Everything; full efficiency + active bonus |
| Homesteader (**idle**) | **Priority list** | Everything; idle efficiency |
| **Auto-harvester** | Nearest in radius | `harvest`, within radius |
| **Sprinkler / drip line** | Not an actor; satisfies `water` tasks in its area (built: drip line, §5.3 and D50) | `water`, area |
| **Mini-tractor / robot** (late) | Its own priority list | Several categories, a zone |
| **The cat** | Its own logic | Mouse control (§7.7) |

*Built (M6, #42): the machine framework and the auto-harvester* (`src/sim/systems/machines.lua`, `Tasks.chooseAreaJob`, knobs `data/tuning/machines.lua`, D65). A placed object with a `machine` definition (capability set of task categories, optional task ids, a radius, a work speed) is a stationary actor: it claims the nearest doable target of its categories within its radius through the same reservations as the homesteader, works it in place, and draws power as a "demand" sink in the machines tier only while it has work. The auto-harvester ($150, Machines chapter) picks ripe crops within 3 tiles.

**Priorities v1:** the idle homesteader has an ordered list of categories (default from data; a category flagged `startsOff` begins Off, as "Clear trees" does, D109). The player can **reorder** and **toggle** them. **Priorities v2 (built, #43, D66):** each category can also carry one optional **condition** ("food below 10 days", "wood below 30", only in a season, from or until an hour); the routine skips a category while its condition is not met and moves on to the next. Later layers on the same list: per-machine lists, rule cards. Within a category, tasks are scored by urgency and **walking distance**. The scoring function is swappable.

### 7.5 Power
- A **network**: sources produce, batteries buffer, sinks draw. Supply/demand → satisfaction ratio. Priority tiers (heating outranks the freezer).
- **The truck is a battery on the network**, the only one at first. Charging for a trip competes with homestead use.
- Sources: folding panel (tiny), solar array, wind, micro-hydro, generator.
- *Built (M5, #34):* the network lives in `src/sim/systems/power.lua` and is content-driven (`data/content/objects.lua`: `source`, `battery`, `sink`; knobs in `data/tuning/power.lua`).
  - **Sources** have an output curve: solar follows the sun's arc and the season (and cloud, via `Weather.powerInputs`), wind follows a seeded hourly wind model and is strongest in winter, micro-hydro is steady but seasonal and must touch the creek, a generator starts only when sinks are short after batteries and burns `fuel`.
  - **Sinks** name a priority tier and a draw in kW. Tiers: heating, pumps, freezer, lights, machines. A sink runs on a schedule: `always`, `dark` (outside daylight), `heat` (dark hours of cold seasons, until the night's warmth is made), `fill` (pumps, while the store has room) or `demand` (machines, while their owner asks, D65). Real sinks today: electric heater (turns kWh into warmth that replaces wood at the night's accounting), work lamp (study speed), chest freezer (slows spoilage). Pumps (#35) and machines (#42) plug into the same tiers.
  - **Priority list** (D46): one ordered list of tiers plus two charge tiers (battery bank, truck). Generation is handed out in list order; a short sink tier is topped up from batteries and then a generator; every sink in a tier gets that tier's satisfaction ratio (`Power.sinkRatio`). The player can reorder the list, switch sinks and the generator off, and stop the truck lending power.
  - **The truck is a battery on the network** (D47). Homestead load is served before the truck charges (unless the player moves "Truck charging" up the list), and the truck lends charge to the homestead only above a trip reserve.
  - **The winter squeeze** is the balance target: a 3 kW array makes about a quarter of its summer energy in winter, wind helps, and a heater plus long dark hours triple the load, so heat, freezer, lights and truck trips compete.
  - UI: a Power panel (button under the speed controls, or tap any panel/battery/device) with generation, load, satisfaction, battery bars, the priority list, sources and a switch per device. Devices show a status light in the world.

### 7.6 The truck
- An **electric pickup**. At run start the battery is flat; it's shelter and storage (the bottom rung of the shelter ladder, §5.3: no insulation, so a cold snap hurts here most; a camp or shack beats it, D82). The **folding solar panel** trickle-charges it.
- **Truck trip** menu (§6.4) when charged enough. No driving gameplay. Tapping the truck at home shows the charge, the load (units, cargo, value) and the spare left at home, with **Load the truck** and **Drive to town** (D72, D75). Driving with an empty truck while goods are spare asks once.
- **Upgrades:** bigger battery, bed rack (cargo: a `cargo` stat modifier adds to `economy.cargoCapacity`, D75; no item yet), trailer (animals, oversized items), bed-mounted solar.

### 7.7 Animals & wildlife

| Animal | Role | Unlock |
|---|---|---|
| **Cat** 🐈 | Companion. Wanders, naps in sunny spots, pettable. **Catches mice.** Without a cat, mice occasionally ruin a crop tile or nibble stores. | Early (cheap, or a stray that shows up) |
| **Fish** | Food, money, collection (§7.10). | Start |
| **Chickens** | Eggs, meat. Need feed, water, coop. Predators at night. | Year 1 |
| **Goats** | Milk → cheese, money. Fencing, shelter, winter hay. | Year 1–2 |
| **Cattle / bulls** | **The big payday.** Sell, or process for a winter of food. | Year 2+ |
| **Wild** | Foraging, trapping. Pests: mice, rabbits, deer, locusts. Predators for poultry. | Varies |

Slaughter / butchering is **off-screen**: a menu action that yields meat.

*Built (M5, #36): mice and the cat* (`src/sim/systems/cat.lua`, knobs `data/tuning/cat.lua`, D54). Each night a seeded roll may send mice to ruin a few units of stored fresh food; a cat (the catalog's Shelter cat, $12, or the stray cat find) catches most raids. The cat wanders, naps in sunny spots, sleeps by the truck at night, eats a fish a day, and can be petted with a tap.

*Built (M6, #40, part one): chickens* (`src/sim/systems/animals.lua`, knobs `data/tuning/animals.lua`, D63, D64). Goats, milk, cheese, hay and the trailer followed (below, D71, #139).
- **Unlock**: the Chicken coop and Hens are journal ideas on the Animals page, written by the notes "First harvest" and "Town trip"; study 2 and 1 nights. The coop ($45, comes with a first sack of feed) is placed on the plot (not on the road); hens ($18 for three) are pickup only, bought on a truck trip, and need a coop with room.
- **The flock** is the ledger resource `hen` (capacity comes from placed coops, six each). Needs: feed from the feeder, water from the waterer, shelter at night. Winter is the pressure: in spring to autumn hens forage for part of their feed, in winter for none, so a winter flock eats more than twice what a summer one does and lays about a third as many eggs.
- **The chore**: the `animals` task category ("Tend animals", after harvest and water in the default routine) has one task, **Tend hens**: collect the eggs from the nest box, top up the feeder from the feed in the ledger, fill the waterer. The idle routine goes when three eggs wait or a bowl is half empty; a tap on the coop does it any time. An active or idle player never needs to do more than that.
- **Eggs** are a food resource (`egg`): they have a keep target and go to the stand and the truck load like any other food (D75). A full egg store keeps them in the nest box (D59); a full box stops the laying and says so.
- **Forgiving but real**: a day with under half the feed or water met is a hungry or dry day; four hungry days (two dry) in a row cost a hen each day after that. A fed day starts the count again.
- **Predators** (D64): once a night a seeded roll may bring a fox; the coop door, the learned Predator fence and the cat turn it back, bad weather makes it likelier; otherwise it takes a hen or two.

*Built (M6, #139): goats, milk, cheese and hay* (same module and knobs, D71). Goats are a second kind on the chicken framework.
- **Unlock**: the Goat pen ($70, comes with 12 hay), Goats ($45 for two) and Hay bales ($6 for ten) are journal ideas on the Animals page. The pen and goats need the notes "First eggs" and "First frost" (chickens first, and the first frost sets the player thinking of winter feed); the Goat trailer ($60, equipment) needs "First eggs" and "Town trip"; Hay bales need the new note "First milk"; the Cheese press ($55) needs "First milk" and "Produce spoils". Goats are pickup only and the market refuses them (`requires = "goat_trailer"`) until the trailer is owned, and until a pen has room.
- **The pen** (`goat_pen`, 2x2, never on the road) houses three goats (ledger resource `goat`, capacity from placed pens), stacks 20 more hay, and holds a pail (8 milk), a manger (18 hay) and a trough (6 water). The **fence is a pen property**: base guard 0.5, plus the goats' own toughness 0.2, plus the learned Predator fence (`predatorGuard`, the same equipment that guards the coop), capped by `maxGuard`.
- **Needs**: hay in the manger (1.5 a goat a day when nothing is browsed; browse covers 15 to 60 percent of it from spring to autumn, none in winter; a cold snap burns half as much again) and water. Winter hay is the pressure: three goats eat 4.5 hay a day in winter, 31 for the season, while giving almost no milk (10 percent a goat-day). Forgiving but real like the hens: five hungry days (three dry) before a goat is lost each further day.
- **Milk** (`milk`, food, category `dairy`, 250 calories, spoils in two days, $3): each goat rolls once a day at 8:00 on its own stream (`milk`) for 1 to 2 units, by season (0.9 spring, 0.85 summer, 0.6 autumn, 0.1 winter), less when underfed or dry and halved in a storm. It waits in the pail; the **Tend goats** chore (`tend_pen`, in the `animals` category, after Tend hens) moves it to the ledger, fills the manger from hay in store and fills the trough. A full milk store holds the milk in the pail (D59) and a full pail says so once a day. The idle routine goes when four milk wait or a bowl is half empty; milk spoils fast, so a tap or the routine should be quick, and the Pantry already shows its rate and tonight's expected loss through the Spoilage accessors.
- **Cheese**: the Cheese press is a processing station; the recipe `make_cheese` takes up to eight milk and gives one cheese per two (recipes gained an optional `yield`) in 12 hours. Cheese is preserved food ($12, 700 calories, never spoils); a placed press makes the pantry hold milk for pressing like fish for the smoker.
- **Predators** reuse the fox roll (D64): one roll a night; with both kinds kept a second draw picks hens or goats (a goat weighs half a hen as prey), a goat raid takes one goat, and a pen is defended by its own guard. Hen-only runs draw exactly as before.
- **Warnings**: a new alert kind `animals` (D70 alerts system) is days of feed (hay or chicken feed, manger plus stock) left: amber under 3 days, red under 1.5, with a toast; the HUD goat chip turns amber or red with it.

### 7.8 Characters (sparse)
The **cat**. **Town** (text only, via the truck and catalog). **Search & rescue** (a recurring rescuer: "You again?"). A **distant neighbor** on the radio (story fragments, weather hints).

**Wardrobe** (D149 to D153, [docs/PLAN_WARDROBE.md](PLAN_WARDROBE.md)). The homesteader has five slots of clothes (head, torso, legs, feet, extra), each garment light, mid or heavy. Light clothes add a little work speed by day and ask a little more heat at night (a full light outfit +4% and +6%); heavy clothes ask less heat (-8%) and add no speed; mid is the baseline and the starter outfit is all mid, so a new slot plays as the game always did. The trade-off only bites in the cold: summer has no warmth need. One outfit applies for a day and the night after it, so a change takes effect at the next dawn (at once between runs and on a run's first day). Garments are earned, never bought, from what the slot has lived through, built and survived, and are kept across runs, per save slot; a Sandbox slot has them all. The menu (§9.4) chooses the outfit, and two saved outfits, **Warm** and **Cold** (the weather each is for), let **Auto** dress one at dawn by the forecast (D156): with Auto on, a coming night that needs warmth of at least `tuning.wardrobe.autoColdNeed` (the weather system's own warmth figure, the one the readiness forecast reads) puts on the Cold outfit, any other night the Warm one. Auto only ever wears a saved outfit and never edits one, does nothing while the outfit it needs is empty, and is off in a new slot, so it changes no balance until the player turns it on.

### 7.9 Events & threats
- Data-driven random events weighted by season, year, and run seed: drought, early frost, hail, blight, locusts, storm damage, mice, predators, a stray cat.
- **Year 2+ threats** are scheduled escalations with countermeasures to learn and buy.

*Built (M6, #41, #97): growing-season threats* (`src/sim/systems/threats.lua`, content `data/content/threats.lua`, curves `data/tuning/threats.lua`, D67). Each year rolls a schedule from its own seeded stream: year 1 one gentle threat from summer on, then more and stronger threats each year, and longer cold snaps on top of the winter severity growth (D37).
- **Hail** (weather-driven): a few days of "hail season" in summer; each day it may strike, far likelier on a storm day (`Weather.threat`), and the forecast says "hail likely tomorrow" when a storm is coming. It knocks crops back. Counter: **Hail netting**.
- **Drought**: a few summer days when beds dry several times faster; crops left dry too long wither; rain breaks it. Counter: **Straw mulch**.
- **Locusts** (year 2+): one day, a share of the crops is stripped. Counter: **Row covers**.
- **Early frost**: one autumn morning kills a share of unripe crops; ripe ones are fine. Counter: **Frost cloth**.
- Each threat is announced a few days ahead (toast and a forecast pill), and ends with a summary toast ("Locusts passed: 4 crops lost, 3 saved by row covers"). Its first strike writes a Field Journal note that makes its countermeasure an idea, so the knowledge carries into the next run. The countermeasures are equipment bought once a run. With nothing in the ground a threat comes and goes without effect; the routine replants lost crops.

### 7.10 Fishing

A **headline activity**: a satisfying skill toy and a collection.

- **Tension meter mini-game (active):**
  1. **Cast**, then wait for a bite (a bobber dip and a sound cue; react in time to hook it).
  2. **Fight:** a tension gauge. **Hold** to reel (gains line, raises tension). **Release** to let the fish run (lowers tension, loses line). The fish has **stamina** and **surges**: reel when it tires, ease off when it pulls.
  3. Tension maxed too long → the fish **slips the hook** (gentle: no lost gear). Fully reeled in → caught.
  4. **Single input** (hold / release), so it works the same with mouse, key, or touch.
- **Gear** raises max tension, reel speed, and bite chance. **Fish** differ in strength, stamina, and surge pattern.
- **Idle:** auto-fishes at a lower rate, common fish only. **Rare fish require active play.**
- **Variety:** species vary by **spot** (creek, pond, lake, ice hole), **season**, **time of day**, **weather**, and **bait**.
- **Fish guide:** collection log. A first catch of each species writes a **Note** (§8.1).
- **Uses:** eat, **smoke** (preserves for winter, sells for more), sell.
- **Winter:** ice fishing.

### 7.11 Save slots
- **3 slots.** Each slot = its own knowledge (journal) + the current run.
- **Renameable**; the list shows **name, year, season, total playtime**.
- Autosave on a timer and on focus loss / suspend.
- **Compatibility stamp** (D74): every save carries `SAVE_COMPAT`. A save from another version is never loaded or deleted by itself: the slot screen offers Delete save (confirmed) or Keep it. No migrations before launch.
- **Sandbox slots** (D134): when a new homestead is named the player chooses Normal or Sandbox. A sandbox slot is the normal game with nothing costing resources and no way to fail; it is tagged SANDBOX in the slot list, the Catalog and the homestead screen.

## 8. Knowledge: the Field Journal

### 8.1 Flow

```
 DO THINGS ─► NOTES ─► IDEAS ─► STUDY (nights) ─► KNOWN ─► catalog item buyable / perk active
             (what     (what you  (you choose     (permanent,
             happened)  could try) which one)      every future run)
```

| Stage | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Note** | A specific thing you experienced. Written automatically by game events. One icon + one short line. | 🐟 *"Fish spoil in 2 days"* (triggered when a fish spoils) |
| **Idea** | A catalog item or perk whose **required Notes** you now have. Appears on the journal map as a sketch. | 💡 **Smoker** (needs: *first catch* + *fish spoil*) |
| **Study** | You choose one Idea to study. It progresses **at night** while the homesteader sleeps. Longer nights in winter = faster studying. A desk / lamp speeds it up. | Smoker: 2 nights |
| **Known** | Permanent. Catalog items become buyable; perks take effect. | Smoker in the catalog → order it → delivered tomorrow |

- **Nothing is spent.** Notes stay. One Note can feed several Ideas.
- **Choosing what to study is a decision**, so it's never automated. If nothing is queued, a gentle prompt shows. The **study queue** holds a few Ideas in order.
- **Notes and Known items persist** across runs (per slot). A study in progress when you fail finishes during recovery (§4.3).
- **How it's built (M3):** Notes are content (`data/content/notes.lua`): an event name, an optional field match, and an optional count ("10 fish caught"). Catalog entries list the Notes they need and their study length in nights. Study progress accrues while the homesteader is in bed: from `tuning.actors.eveningHours` after the season's sunset (`tuning.time.daylight`) until sunrise. One "night" is `tuning.study.nightHours` hours in bed, so a long winter night is worth more than one study night and a short summer night less (D34, D35). Equipment with a `studySpeed` modifier (desk, lamp) speeds it up.

### 8.2 What can be known

- **Catalog items**: gear, buildings, seeds, animals, machines.
- **Perks**:
  - **Starting gear**: "pack a better rod / an axe / a seed tin next time."
  - **Head starts**: truck partly charged, a little starting food.
  - **Habits** (idle): shorter idle takeover, better idle efficiency, more priority options.
  - **Weather sense**: earlier or more accurate forecasts.
  - **Discoveries**: new crops, fish, animals, events in future runs.
- *Built (M3):* perks are content (`data/content/perks.lua`) sharing one id space with the catalog as journal nodes. Run-start effects (`startingResources`, `startingEquipment`, `truckCharge`) are handed out once per run by `src/sim/systems/perks.lua`; `modifiers` (`idleTakeover`, `idleEfficiency`, `forecastLead`, ...) apply through `src/sim/stats.lua` like equipment. Discoveries are not in yet.

### 8.3 Journal UI: a visual map, not text

The journal is a **knowledge map**, not pages of prose. It's an interactive layout of nodes:

```
   ┌─ FISHING ───────────────────────────┐   ┌─ PRESERVING ─────────┐
   │  [🐟]──┐                            │   │                      │
   │  [🎣]──┼──► ( Better rod ✓ )        │   │  ( Smoker ✎ 1/2 )    │
   │  [🌙]──┘          │                 │   │    ▲     ▲           │
   │                   ▼                 │   │  [🐟]  [🦠]          │
   │            ( Fly reel ? )           │   └──────────────────────┘
   └─────────────────────────────────────┘
   [🐟] = Note stamp    ( ) = node    ✓ known   ✎ studying   💡 idea   ? unknown
```

- **Nodes** (items and perks) are grouped by **chapter regions**: Water & Soil, Power, Fishing, Animals, Preserving, Shelter, Weather, Habits, Machines.
- **Notes are small icon stamps** with **lines** to the nodes they unlock. Seeing which experiences led where *is* the tech tree.
- **Node states are visual**: unknown (faint "?" with silhouette), idea (pencil sketch), studying (progress ring), known (inked and colored), owned this run (badge).
- **Tap a node** → compact card: icon, name, one-line effect, required Note stamps (filled / empty), study time, price. Buttons: *Study* / *Buy*.
- **Tap a Note** → its one line plus when you got it. Longer flavor text is optional behind a "read more" tap.
- **Journal and catalog share the same nodes.** The catalog is the same data as a shop view (known items, prices, specials, cart). One data model, two views.
- **Text budget:** labels ≤ ~3 words, effect lines ≤ ~8 words, no paragraphs in menus.
- *Built (M3, navigation reworked by D81):* `src/scenes/journal.lua` shows a chapter overview and one fitted chapter at a time (no free panning; see D81). Chapters are content (`data/content/chapters.lua`: each lists the node `page`s it holds, empty chapters are hidden) and `src/ui/journal_map.lua` lays them out: each chapter is a region with its note stamps on the left, its nodes on the right and a line from each stamp to the nodes that need it; regions are packed into columns in chapter order, each column as tall as the map viewport. A study-queue strip sits above the map (with the "nothing queued" prompt) and a notes strip below it (tap a stamp for its line and when and in which run it was written). Tapping a node opens the card over the bottom of the map: icon, name, effect line, needed stamps (filled / blank), study nights, price, and *Study* / *Remove* / *Order* (known catalog items go through `Market.order`, so the catalog and the journal stay one data model). Starter items show as known nodes with no stamps. Unknown nodes show a silhouette and "???" and never their name.

### 8.4 Run variety (lean in, pull back if needed)
Land (§7.3), forecast and threat order, weekly catalog specials, "pick 1 of 3" finds, random events. All randomness uses a **seeded RNG per run** (reproducible for testing and bug reports).

*Built (M5, #38): finds* (`src/sim/systems/finds.lua`, content `data/content/finds.lua`, knobs `data/tuning/finds.lua`, modal `src/scenes/finds.lua`, D53). A daily seeded roll queues an offer, shown as a modal of three distinct finds when the homesteader is not resting or fishing; pick one and its data effects apply.

## 9. Presentation

### 9.1 View: top-down 3/4
Slight 3/4 perspective (Stardew-style). Camera pans and zooms. Not isometric for now. Grid-only sim keeps that option open.

### 9.2 Art & audio
- Pixel art, **16×16 tiles (tentative)**, a single config value. Virtual resolution ~640×360, integer-scaled.
- Asset packs and/or custom art. **Placeholders first.** All sprites go through an **asset manifest**, so swapping art never touches game code.
- Strong palette shifts by season and time of day. Winter looks cold, quiet, and blue.
- **Art direction (D136): clarity and depth, a little drama.** Thronefall is the reference for strong light and readable shapes. The rules: light, calm open ground and deep, cool foliage and objects; the homesteader's red kept rare; everything lit from the same top-left sun (a lit edge on the sun side, a cool form shade on the far side and underneath); shadows that tint the ground cool rather than grey it; objects that sit in the ground (ambient occlusion); foliage drawn as lit clumps, not speckle; woods that read as one shaded area.
- **The homesteader (D153)**: a 20x22 mascot chibi (big head, about two heads tall), one static frame, painted from parts in `src/render/character.lua` in the world's three-step, top-left-lit, ink-outlined style, and dressed by garment `look` fields (`Character.outfitFor`). The red always travels: every outfit keeps a saturated red accent.
- **Shaders carry the ambience.** A big share of the game's mood and texture comes from LÖVE shaders: animated water, time-of-day color grading, film grain, and later wind, cloud shadows, night lights, rain, snow and frost, mist, and fireflies. They stay pixel-art friendly (snapped to the art-pixel grid, banded rather than smooth). Parameters live in tuning data and reload live (F6). The full list is in [docs/ASSETS.md](ASSETS.md).
- Audio: nature ambience, crackling stove, inverter hum, purring cat, sparse music, a great fish-on sound. Built as one cue registry with synthesized placeholders and drop-in asset replacement (D100, [docs/AUDIO.md](AUDIO.md)).

### 9.3 Desktop companion mode (first playable, Windows only)
A toggle into a small, borderless, **always-on-top**, draggable window. **View-only**: a framed view of the homestead plus minimal status (season, day, winter readiness, pending decisions). Built with LuaJIT FFI → Win32. Hidden on platforms that don't support it.

**Look (D142).**
- **Plates lit like the world.** A plate is frosted glass: the graded world behind it, blurred, cut into a few levels with an ordered dither on the UI pixel grid, pulled toward a dark warm base. - **Calm signals.** Amber (needs you) gets a single-pixel glint every few seconds instead of a pulsing border; red (danger) a slow three-frame beat. Nothing flashes.

- **Books are paper.** Paper fibre on the art-pixel grid, ink-stamp icons with a one-pixel misregistration; at night the page is lit by a warm light pool.

- **Icons** sit on the 12×12 icon grid of docs/ASSETS.md, shown at 1x on the HUD and 2x in the wheel, lit from the top left with a warm dark outline. Colour is kept for meaning: amber needs you, red is danger, blue is water, warm yellow is power, and the UI uses no green (the world is green).

Keyboard: the chips and Save are focus-grid members (zones actions and presets), Enter presses the focused one.

## 12. Technical architecture (modularity pillar)

### 12.1 Principles
1. **Definitions vs. state.** Content lives in data files. Runtime state is plain tables that serialize directly. Systems never hard-code content IDs.
2. **Systems talk through shared interfaces only:** the **resource ledger**, the **task board**, and the **event bus**. A system's slice of `world.state` is private: other systems read it only through small accessor functions on the owner (`Food.crateOf`, `Crops.tileInUse`, `Land.truck`, `Actors.list`). `tests/test_architecture.lua` enforces this per state key; the grid is the one shared structure.
3. **Simulation is separate from presentation.** Fixed tick, no LÖVE graphics calls in the sim. Grid coordinates only; projection lives in the renderer.
4. **Components over inheritance.**
5. **No magic numbers in systems.** Every balance value lives in data (§12.3).
6. **Seeded randomness** through a per-run RNG service.
7. **Meta vs. run state are separate.** Slot = `{ meta = knowledge..., run = current run... }`. Failing discards `run`, keeps `meta`. A breaking change to either shape bumps `SAVE_COMPAT` (D74); there are no save migrations before launch.
8. **Notes are event-driven.** Systems emit events (`fish.caught`, `food.spoiled`); the journal listens and writes Notes based on data rules. Systems never know the journal exists.
9. **If adding a crop requires touching more than one data file, that's a smell.**

### 12.2 Module layout

```
game/
  main.lua                 -- LÖVE callbacks → app
  conf.lua
  src/
    core/                  -- engine-ish, game-agnostic
      app.lua              -- scene stack, virtual resolution, lifecycle
      events.lua           -- pub/sub event bus
      clock.lua            -- fixed-step sim clock, time scale
      registry.lua         -- loads & validates content definitions
      tuning.lua           -- loads tuning, strict access
      rng.lua              -- seeded RNG streams
      save.lua             -- slots, serialize/deserialize, autosave
      input.lua            -- raw input → actions; idle timer
      camera.lua           -- pan/zoom, grid↔screen projection
      platform.lua         -- capability flags, OS hooks (widget mode)
      assets.lua           -- asset manifest, sprite lookup
    sim/                   -- game rules, no rendering
      world.lua            -- owns run state, ticks systems in order
      systems/             -- calendar, ledger, grid, landgen, tasks, actors, crops,
                           -- fishing, food, processing, animals, power, needs,
                           -- threats, market, delivery, ...
      components/
    meta/                  -- rescue, run setup (the journal itself is sim/systems/journal.lua, state in meta)
    ui/                    -- HUD, panels, journal map, catalog, widgets
    scenes/                -- title, slots, homestead, fishing, truck trip, journal, rescue, widget
  data/
    content/               -- WHAT exists: crops, fish, buildings, animals, items, notes, events
    tuning/                -- HOW MUCH: all balance knobs
  lib/                     -- third-party (minimal)
  assets/
tests/                     -- headless sim tests + balance harness
```

### 12.3 Tuning & balance data
1. **Per-thing values live with the thing** in `data/content/` (a carrot's grow time, seed price, sell price, and Note requirements are all in the carrot's definition).
2. **Global knobs** live in `data/tuning/<domain>.lua`: `time`, `idle`, `needs`, `economy`, `study`, `fishing`, `threats`, `power`, `animals`...
3. **No numeric literals in system code.** Systems read `tuning.idle.takeoverDelay`, never `30`.
4. **Strict access.** Unknown keys error at load. Typos never silently become `nil`.
5. **Every value commented** with unit and intent.
6. **Curves** (winter severity per year, price drift) are tables or small formulas in tuning.
7. Later: **hot-reload** tuning files and an in-game **tuning panel**.
8. The **balance harness** reads the same files.

### 12.4 Libraries
Minimal, vendored in `game/lib/`. Prefer small in-house modules for core systems.

### 12.5 Testing
Headless sim tests, plus a **balance harness** that simulates years with scripted, idle-only, and active-like play, reporting survival odds, money, and knowledge curves.
